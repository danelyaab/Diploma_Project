# User discovery: interview protocol and results

## Status
**Three participant interviews are represented in the notes below (P01–P03).** The team confirmed the date and format as **20 October 2026, in person** for all three interviews. Consent to publish the anonymized notes was not specified and must be confirmed. Findings are preliminary and exploratory; they are not representative of all potential users. The minimum of three discussions is met, while one or two additional discussions may broaden the evidence.

## Method and protocol
P01–P03 were conducted in person on 20 October 2026, as confirmed by the team. For any additional discussions, use short semi-structured conversations with adults who use a smartphone. Ask about current activity tracking, missing readings, goals, reminder preferences, data permissions/privacy, and accessibility. Use open-ended questions and avoid leading participants toward MoveWell features. Participation should be voluntary. Do not collect names, email addresses, diagnoses, or identifying health details in the public repository.

## Interview guide
1. How do you currently keep track of physical activity, if at all?
2. Tell me about the last time you wanted to check your activity history.
3. What makes an activity tracker useful or frustrating for you?
4. How do you feel about manually entering an activity when the phone has no record?
5. What would you want to know about where a step or activity value came from?
6. Would reminders be useful? When would they be unwanted?
7. What concerns would you have about connecting an app to phone or wearable health data?
8. Which information would you want to see on the first screen?
9. Is there anything important that I have not asked?

### Interview 1 (P01)

**Interviewer:** How do you currently track your physical activity, if at all?

**Participant:** I don’t track it at the moment because I haven’t found a suitable app.

**Interviewer:** When was the last time you wanted to look back at your activity history?

**Participant:** Yesterday, after I went to the gym.

**Interviewer:** What do you find convenient about the way you currently track your activity, and what gets in the way?

**Interviewer:** Oh, so you don’t track it at the moment. Got it.

**Interviewer:** What do you do if your data is missing or seems inaccurate? For example, if your phone automatically counts your steps.

**Participant:** I’d like to add the activity myself, because I know I’ve done more—for example, if I’ve been walking, cycling, or working out at the gym.

**Interviewer:** Would you like to enter your activities manually?

*[No separate answer was recorded; the participant had just said they would prefer to add activities themselves.]*

**Interviewer:** How important are reminders to you? When would they be inconvenient?

**Participant:** An evening reminder after work would probably be helpful. After a busy day, I might forget, so it would be good to get a reminder in the evening.

**Interviewer:** What data would you be comfortable connecting or entering, and what would you prefer not to share?

**Participant:** I’d be comfortable sharing health data, such as my heart rate during physical activity. I’d like to limit it to that.

**Interviewer:** What would you like to see on the home screen?

**Participant:** My goal, how active I’ve been today, and how much more activity I should do to reach my goal. For example, if I want to lose a certain amount of weight, I’d like to know how much I should walk, run, or exercise each day.

**Interviewer:** Great, thank you for your answers.

### Interview 2 (P02)

**Interviewer:** How do you currently track your physical activity, if at all?

**Participant:** I currently track it using the Apple Health app, which counts my steps and tracks a few other things.

**Interviewer:** When was the last time you wanted to look back at your activity history?

**Participant:** A few days ago, when I ran a marathon.

**Interviewer:** What do you find convenient about the way you track your activity now, and what’s missing?

**Participant:** It does a good job of counting my steps. But I feel like it could have more features to track other aspects of my health and help me reach a specific goal, such as losing weight or gaining muscle.

**Interviewer:** What do you do if your data is missing or seems inaccurate?

**Participant:** Sometimes I feel that the app counts my steps incorrectly, or misses part of my activity, but I don’t do anything about it. I just leave it as it is.

**Interviewer:** Would you like to enter activities manually? If so, when?

**Participant:** Yes. I’d like to add activities that the app doesn’t track, such as swimming, running a race, or going to a class. I also cycle often, so it would be useful to see how many kilometres I’ve cycled. I’d like it to track how many floors I’ve climbed by taking the stairs, too. I often choose the stairs.

**Interviewer:** How important are reminders to you? When would they be useful?

**Participant:** They could be useful on days when I haven’t been very active, to remind me to get moving. They wouldn’t be useful if they came at the wrong time. Otherwise, I wouldn’t mind them as simple reminders.

**Interviewer:** What data would you be comfortable connecting or entering, and what would you prefer not to share?

**Participant:** I’d be comfortable connecting health and fitness information such as my weight, height, and general health details. If the app were connected to a smartwatch, I’d also be open to it recording my heart rate and how regular my breathing is. I don’t mind sharing my location, because I understand it may be needed to calculate distance. It could also help with step tracking. I wouldn’t want to share personal documents or other private information, but I don’t think the app would need those anyway.

**Interviewer:** What would you like to see on the home screen?

**Participant:** I’d like to see how many steps I’ve taken today, my daily step goal, and what percentage of the goal I’ve reached. It would also be helpful to see how many steps I’ve taken during the week, my heart rate, and the activities I’ve done that day, such as running, cycling, or snowboarding.

For women, it might also be useful to see their menstrual cycle phase. On some days, the app could suggest a lower activity goal, since physical activity can feel more difficult then.

**Interviewer:** Great, thank you for your answers.

### Interview 3 (P03)

**Interviewer:** How do you currently track your physical activity, if at all?

**Participant:** At the moment, I mainly use two apps. The first is Fitness, the standard app on iOS. It tracks my steps, calories burned, and so on. I also use an app from the gym I go to. It shows my visits, how often I go, how much time I spend at the gym, and other activity details. I really like it; it’s very convenient.

**Interviewer:** When was the last time you wanted to look back at your activity history?

**Participant:** I use it every day, actually. I’m trying to take better care of my health and go to the gym more often, so I actively check the app every day.

**Interviewer:** What do you find convenient about your current approach, and what gets in the way?

**Participant:** The interface makes it easy to keep everything under control. It’s convenient to have everything at hand. The downside is that it depends a lot on my phone. For example, if my battery runs out, I can’t use the app.

**Interviewer:** What do you do if your data is missing or seems inaccurate?

**Participant:** Usually, I can edit the data myself. I can add or remove information if needed.

**Interviewer:** Would you like to enter activities manually? If so, when?

**Participant:** Yes, I like entering them manually. I find it more accurate and convenient than automated tracking because I know exactly what I’ve done, how much time I spent, and how many calories I burned.

**Interviewer:** How important are reminders to you? When would they be inconvenient?

**Participant:** I don’t really use reminders. If something is important, I usually remember it myself. But I do sometimes pay attention to notifications.

**Interviewer:** What data would you be comfortable connecting or entering, and what would you prefer not to share?

**Participant:** There’s some information I’d rather not share, such as my passport or ID details. But I’m fairly relaxed about it. If the information is required, I usually fill it in, and I don’t find that particularly troublesome.

**Interviewer:** What would you like to see on the home screen?

**Participant:** I have the activity-tracking app as a large widget on my phone’s home screen, alongside my other basic apps. Inside the app, I’d like to see how many steps I’ve taken and what activity I’ve done over a given period. I especially like seeing my statistics and graphs for the year, or across the past two or three years.

**Interviewer:** Great, thank you for your answers.

## Preliminary synthesis (three participants)

These are exploratory findings from three participants, not population estimates. Themes reported by more than one participant are:

| Theme | Evidence in these interviews | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Manual activity entry and correction | All three wanted to add activity manually; P01 and P03 explicitly described correcting or supplementing automated records, while P02 reported missed or inaccurate steps | Keep FR-02 as Must; support clear activity type, date/time, duration, and correction of a saved manual entry. Label the source of each record. |
| A useful summary, goals, and history | All three wanted an easy view of activity and progress; P01 and P02 mentioned daily goals, and P02/P03 asked for weekly or longer-term history | Keep FR-03, FR-04, and FR-05 as Must. Show today's activity, a user-set target and progress, plus at least seven days of history; consider longer charts only after scope review. |
| Activities beyond step counts | All three described workouts or other activity they want represented; P02 specifically named swimming, cycling, races, and stairs | Ensure manual logs support more than steps. Do not promise automatic measurement of every activity type. |
| Privacy and data boundaries | Participants differed in the health data they would share; all expressed at least one boundary or limitation around personal information | Keep data minimization and explicit permission in NFR-01. Do not request identity documents. Treat location and health metrics as optional integrations requiring a separate scope/privacy decision. |
| Reminders | P01 preferred an evening reminder, P02 wanted context-sensitive prompts on low-activity days, and P03 does not routinely use reminders | Keep reminders optional and configurable (FR-06 Should); avoid default or poorly timed notifications. |

Individual feature requests—not established recurring needs—include weight-loss or muscle-gain recommendations (P01/P02), heart rate and other health measures (P01/P02), menstrual-cycle features (P02), and multi-year charts (P03). Do not add health or weight-loss prescriptions. The MVP should support user-defined goals and show progress without claiming a medically appropriate target. Confirm any health-data or location integration with the supervisor and through a privacy review.
